## Verbatim Final Spoken Narration

Twenty meters beneath Greenland's frozen surface, trillions of liters of liquid water never freeze. Across thousands of square kilometers, a hidden reservoir flows inside solid polar ice.

Even through minus thirty degree winter nights, this buried water stays liquid inside porous snow. Until 2011, ice models assumed summer meltwater simply refroze near the surface.

While drilling cores near Helheim Glacier, field teams pulled up dripping wet snow from twenty meters down. Heavy autumn snowfall acts like an insulating blanket, sealing summer warmth inside.

Airborne radar flights and ground sensors then uncovered a deeper surprise. That buried water does not stay locked in place. It drains downward through deep crevasses toward the glacier bed.

At the bedrock, pressurized water lubricates the ice base and speeds glacier motion toward dark coastal fjords. Freshwater plumes then rise from hundreds of meters below the sea surface.

When mineral-rich meltwater enters the North Atlantic, ocean expeditions track how it stirs surface nutrients above the deep twilight zone. Hidden glacier plumbing shapes marine food webs miles offshore.

Glaciologists are still studying how fast these aquifers drain into the sea, so long-term sea-level forecasts remain an open question. Beneath solid white ice, a dark river keeps moving.
