## Verbatim Final Spoken Narration

In East Antarctica, crimson water pours straight out of solid white glacier ice. Even at minus seventeen degrees Celsius, the flow never freezes.

In 1911, early explorers blamed red surface algae. Chemical sampling decades later ruled algae out completely and pointed to dissolved iron.

Ice-penetrating radar traced the brine to hypersaline seawater trapped four hundred meters underground. High salt content lowers its freezing point, and dissolved iron rusts red when it meets air.

Researchers are still studying how completely sealed those deep microbes remain, yet they survive in total darkness without oxygen.
