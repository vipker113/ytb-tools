Why is Venus hotter than Mercury, even though it's farther from the Sun? Closer Mercury hits four hundred thirty degrees Celsius by day, but with almost no atmosphere, that heat escapes into space at night, plunging to minus one hundred eighty degrees. Venus looks like it should be cooler, because thick pale clouds of sulfuric acid reflect about seventy-five percent of sunlight. Yet the sunlight that gets through warms the rocky surface, and its dense carbon dioxide atmosphere, ninety-six point five percent pure, blocks outgoing infrared heat. While Mercury cools every night, Venus stays locked near four hundred sixty-four degrees Celsius, day and night.
