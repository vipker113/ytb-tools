## 3. Factual Scientific References

  *Supports:* Goddard's 1926 liquid oxygen/gasoline rocket lacked an aerodynamic fuselage or nose cone, placing the combustion chamber and nozzle at the top above naked fuel lines and suspended tanks.
- **NASA History Division — Vanguard TV-3 Launch Failure (Dec 6, 1957), Juno I / Explorer 1 Spinning Solid-Rocket Cluster (Jan 31, 1958), and Mercury-Atlas Pressurized Stainless-Steel Balloon Tanks (1962).** — https://www.nasa.gov/history/  
  *Supports:* Vanguard TV-3 rose ~4 feet before collapsing into flames; Juno I spun an upper-stage tub of 11 scaled-down Sergeant rockets; Atlas used <1 mm pressurized stainless-steel balloon tanks and 1.5-stage side booster staging.
- **NASA Marshall Space Flight Center & Kennedy Space Center — Saturn IB (8 Redstone/Jupiter tanks & 8 H-1 engines), Saturn V (363 ft, five F-1 engines at 7.5M lbf), Space Shuttle RS-25, and Artemis Space Launch System (four RS-25s + twin SRBs at 8.8M lbf).** — https://www.nasa.gov/reference/space-launch-system/  
  *Supports:* Saturn IB clustered eight narrow tanks over eight H-1 engines; Saturn V stood 36 stories with five 3.7 m F-1 nozzles generating 7.5M lbf; Space Shuttle paired a ceramic-tiled orbiter with an external tank and 3 RS-25s; Artemis SLS generates 8.8M lbf while propellant remains ~88–90% of launch mass.
