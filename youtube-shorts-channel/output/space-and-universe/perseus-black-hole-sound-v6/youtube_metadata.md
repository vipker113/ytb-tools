## YouTube Description (Ready to Copy-Paste — Includes Required Media Credits)

Most people learn that no sound can travel through space. Across empty interstellar vacuum, that remains completely true—yet inside giant galaxy clusters, NASA's Chandra X-ray Observatory discovered the deepest acoustic sound waves ever detected in the universe.

Roughly 250 million light-years from Earth, the Perseus galaxy cluster (centered on active giant galaxy NGC 1275) is enveloped in superheated 10-million-degree X-ray-emitting plasma spanning hundreds of thousands of light-years. In 2003, astronomers led by Prof. Andrew Fabian at the University of Cambridge analyzed 53 hours of Chandra X-ray data and discovered concentric pressure ripples ~30,000 light-years wide rolling outward through the cluster gas—driven by relativistic black hole jets inflating giant cavities.

Those ripples are true acoustic sound waves oscillating at a deep B-flat roughly 57 octaves below middle C—about 144 quadrillion times lower than human ears can detect, taking nearly 10 million years per single oscillation. In 2022, NASA extracted Chandra's radial X-ray pressure waves and resynthesized them upward by 57 to 58 octaves into human hearing, while revealing how those colossal acoustic waves heat the cluster gas for billions of years and prevent runaway star collapse.
