## Verbatim Final Spoken Narration

You were taught that space is completely silent—until NASA captured the real sound of a black hole.

Two hundred fifty million light-years away sits the Perseus galaxy cluster. While empty space is a silent vacuum, this giant cluster is wrapped in superheated plasma spanning hundreds of thousands of light-years.

In 2003, astronomers studying NASA's Chandra X-ray images noticed giant concentric ripples rolling through that gas. Relativistic jets blasting from the central black hole were inflating enormous cavities—pushing true sound waves outward.

The pitch hit a deep B-flat fifty-seven octaves below middle C. Each oscillation takes nearly ten million years—one hundred forty-four quadrillion times deeper than human ears can detect.

Telescopes show no evidence of sound traveling through empty vacuum. Instead, NASA extracted Chandra's real X-ray pressure ripples and scaled them up fifty-seven octaves—revealing this haunting cosmic roar.

Those acoustic waves carry colossal energy across the cluster, heating the surrounding gas for billions of years so trillions of stars cannot chaotically collapse.

The universe isn't silent after all—we simply needed an X-ray telescope to hear its deepest note.
