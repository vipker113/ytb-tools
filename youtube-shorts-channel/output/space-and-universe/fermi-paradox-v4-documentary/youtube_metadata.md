## YouTube Description (Ready to Copy-Paste — Includes Required Media Credits)

NASA's Kepler space telescope data indicates our Milky Way galaxy holds over 300 million rocky planets orbiting inside the liquid-water habitable zones of sun-like stars—and billions of those star systems formed billions of years before our Sun ignited (Bryson et al., The Astronomical Journal, 2021).

Even traveling at just 1% of light speed, a spacefaring civilization with self-replicating probes or starships could cross the entire Milky Way in roughly 10 million years—a brief blink in a 13-billion-year-old galaxy (Hart, QJRAS, 1975; Fermi, 1950 / Jones, LA-10311-MS, 1985). Yet since Frank Drake's Project Ozma began scanning nearby stars in 1960, over six decades of radio and optical SETI observations have detected zero confirmed extraterrestrial signals.

Economist Robin Hanson (1998) framed this contradiction as "The Great Filter": somewhere between lifeless rock and galaxy-spanning starflight sits at least one extreme evolutionary or technological bottleneck that almost nothing survives. Either that filter lies behind us—such as Earth's nearly two-billion-year microbial stall before two simple prokaryotes merged via endosymbiosis into complex eukaryotic cells—or it lies ahead of us, if technological civilizations routinely destroy themselves before reaching the stars. Crucially, radio silence does not prove we are alone: astronomers calculate that all SETI surveys combined have sampled only a tiny fraction—often compared to a single glass of water—from the vast multi-dimensional cosmic ocean (Wright, Kanodia, & Lubar, The Astronomical Journal, 2018).
