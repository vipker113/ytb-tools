## YouTube Description (Ready to Copy-Paste — Includes Required Media Credits)

How did NASA's Parker Solar Probe plunge straight into the Sun's outer atmosphere—where plasma temperatures exceed 1,000,000°C—without melting?

Crossing below the Alfvén critical surface and swooping within 3.8 million miles (6.1 million km) of the solar photosphere, Parker Solar Probe captured direct white-light WISPR camera sequences of coronal streamers rushing past the spacecraft. In space physics, temperature measures how fast individual particles move, while heat transfer depends on particle density: because the upper solar corona is billions of times more tenuous than sea-level air, relatively few superheated ions strike the spacecraft at once.

Facing intense solar radiation, a 4.5-inch (11.4 cm) carbon-composite foam Thermal Protection System—97% empty space coated in reflective white alumina—peaks near 1,370°C (2,500°F) on its sunlit face while keeping scientific instruments behind it near a comfortable 30°C (85°F).
