  *Supports:* Multi-year winter track and wildlife census showing elk, roe deer, wild boar, Przewalski's horses, and gray wolves thriving in the Chernobyl Exclusion Zone after human evacuation, with wolf density ~7x higher than surrounding reserves.
- **Dadachova, E., Bryan, R. A., Huang, X., Moadel, T., Schweitzer, A. D., Aisen, P., Nosanchuk, J. D., & Casadevall, A. (2007). Ionizing radiation changes the electronic properties of melanin and enhances the growth of melanized fungi. PLOS ONE, 2(5), e457.** (`DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0000457`) — https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0000457  
  *Supports:* Melanin-rich fungi including Cladosporium sphaerospermum isolated from Chernobyl reactor walls exhibit directional radiotropism and enhanced metabolic growth under ionizing gamma radiation.
- **Bastian, F., Jurado, V., Nováková, A., Alabouvette, C., & Saiz-Jimenez, C. (2010). The microbiology of Lascaux Cave. Microbiology, 156(3), 644–652.** (`DOI: 10.1099/mic.0.036160-0`) — https://doi.org/10.1099/mic.0.036160-0  
  *Supports:* Visitor respiration (CO2, heat, water vapor), artificial lighting, and tracked organic microbes disrupted Lascaux Cave's microclimate, causing green algae ('maladie verte') and white Fusarium solani fungal outbreaks that forced permanent public closure and construction of replica caves.
- **Magnússon, B., Magnússon, S. H., Ólafsson, E., & Sigurdsson, B. D. (2014). Plant colonization, succession and ecosystem development on Surtsey with reference to neighbouring islands. Biogeosciences, 11, 5521–5537.** (`DOI: 10.5194/bg-11-5521-2014`) — https://doi.org/10.5194/bg-11-5521-2014  
  *Supports:* Strict human quarantine on volcanic Surtsey Island since its 1963 submarine eruption, biosecurity protocols preventing anthropogenic seed introduction (including removal of an accidental human-introduced tomato plant), and seagull-driven nutrient enrichment enabling >60 vascular plant species to colonize barren basalt.
