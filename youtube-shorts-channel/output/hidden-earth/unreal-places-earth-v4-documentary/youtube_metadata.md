## YouTube Description (Ready to Copy-Paste — Includes Required Media Credits)

None of these three places on Earth were made with CGI—and the last one is hot and humid enough to condense water inside human lungs.

At Kawah Ijen volcano in East Java, Indonesia, pressurized sulfur-rich volcanic gas escapes fumarole cracks at up to 600°C, ignites on contact with air, and condenses into burning liquid sulfur that flows down dark rock as electric-blue flames at night (Delmelle et al., Journal of Volcanology and Geothermal Research, 2000).

In northern Ethiopia's Danakil Depression, the Dallol hydrothermal field sits above a shallow magma intrusion boiling hyper-saline, iron-rich sulfuric brines down into negative pH values (below 0), creating neon-green and yellow pools where astrobiology surveys found zero active microbial life despite abundant liquid water (Belilla et al., Nature Ecology & Evolution, 2019).

Three hundred meters beneath Naica in Chihuahua, Mexico, the Cave of the Crystals grew selenite gypsum beams up to 11 meters long and 55 tons over hundreds of thousands of years in mineral-saturated 58°C water (García-Ruiz et al., Geology, 2007; Van Driessche et al., PNAS, 2011). Drained during mining operations, its 58°C air at over 90% humidity is hotter than 37°C human lungs—causing water vapor to condense inside unprotected airways until pumps stopped and groundwater resealed the cavern in 2017.
