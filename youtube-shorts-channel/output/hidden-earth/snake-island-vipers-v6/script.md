## Verbatim Final Spoken Narration

Imagine four thousand deadly pit vipers trapped on one tiny island—hanging from the trees at eye level.

Thirty-three kilometers off Brazil sits Ilha da Queimada Grande—a steep island completely overrun by venomous snakes. The danger is so extreme that Brazil's Navy banned unauthorized civilians from ever stepping ashore.

Viral myths claim five snakes wait on every square meter. Butantan Institute surveys show no evidence of that rumor—yet still count up to ninety deadly vipers per hectare. Walk just one hundred meters through this forest, and dozens of camouflaged vipers surround you.

Even worse, they don't just slither underfoot. In this silent forest, danger hangs directly above your head.

Trapped by rising seas eleven thousand years ago without mammals, thousands of vipers climbed into the trees. Today they hang coiled at neck height along dense branches, waiting to ambush landing birds.

To stop bitten prey from flying out to sea, they evolved venom up to five times more potent. A single strike melts flesh in seconds—dropping birds straight onto the forest floor.

Humans staffed a lighthouse here in 1909—before automating the tower and abandoning the forest to four thousand vipers.

## Factual Sources Supporting the Narration

1. **Marques, O. A. V., Martins, M., Develey, P. F., Macarrão, A., & Sazima, I. (2012) / Instituto Butantan — Population ecology, arboreal foraging, and avian diet of Bothrops insularis on Ilha da Queimada Grande, São Paulo, Brazil.** — https://butantan.gov.br/
   - *Supports:* Total population estimated at ~2,000–4,000 Bothrops insularis across 43 hectares (~50–90 vipers per hectare in forest habitat; no scientific evidence for the 1–5 snakes per m² urban myth); semi-arboreal ambush foraging on migratory passerine birds.
2. **Guimarães, M., Munguía-Steyer, R., Doherty, P. F., Martins, M., & Sawaya, R. J. — Post-glacial sea-level isolation (~11,000 years BP) of Ilha da Queimada Grande 33 km off São Paulo.** — https://www.iucnredlist.org/
   - *Supports:* Rising Holocene sea levels ~11,000 years ago submerged the continental-shelf connection to mainland Brazil, isolating ancestral Bothrops jararaca populations without terrestrial mammalian prey.
3. **Zelanis, A. et al. / Instituto Butantan Toxinology — Differential venom potency of Bothrops insularis toward avian prey.** — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/
   - *Supports:* Bothrops insularis venom exhibits up to fivefold higher lethality against avian prey than mainland relatives, causing rapid immobilization and tissue necrosis so struck birds cannot fly off the island.
4. **Marinha do Brasil / ICMBio — ARIE das Ilhas Queimada Pequena e Queimada Grande Restricted Access & Automated 1909 Lighthouse.** — https://www.gov.br/icmbio/pt-br
   - *Supports:* Civilian landing is prohibited by the Brazilian Navy and ICMBio except for permitted scientific teams and Navy maintenance of the automated 1909 lighthouse.
