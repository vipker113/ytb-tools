## YouTube Description (Ready to Copy-Paste — Includes Required Media Credits)

Thirty-three kilometers off the coast of São Paulo, Brazil, sits Ilha da Queimada Grande—better known as Snake Island—a steep 43-hectare Atlantic rainforest island so densely populated by venomous pit vipers that the Brazilian Navy prohibits unauthorized civilians from landing.

Online rumors often claim five snakes wait on every square meter. Field surveys by researchers from São Paulo's Instituto Butantan show no evidence of that exaggerated myth, yet confirm an extraordinary real density: roughly 2,000 to 4,000 golden lancehead pit vipers (Bothrops insularis) across the island, reaching up to 50 to 90 deadly vipers per hectare in prime forest habitat.

Isolated roughly 11,000 years ago when rising post-glacial seas flooded the land bridge to mainland Brazil, the trapped vipers ran out of ground mammals and adapted to hunt migratory birds in the canopy. Today they hang coiled along branches at neck and eye height, armed with fast-acting hemotoxic venom up to five times more potent against avian prey than mainland relatives—preventing bitten birds from flying out to sea before collapsing. Since the island's 1909 lighthouse was automated in the 1920s, no humans live permanently on Queimada Grande.
