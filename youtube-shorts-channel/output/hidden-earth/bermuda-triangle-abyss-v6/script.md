## Verbatim Final Spoken Narration

Beneath the southern edge of the Bermuda Triangle, the Atlantic seafloor plunges more than eight thousand meters into pitch-black abyss.

That chasm is the Puerto Rico Trench—the deepest point in the entire Atlantic Ocean. Before modern sonar and deep-sea robots, any ship sinking over trenches this deep simply vanished beyond human reach—turning tragic wrecks into legends.

Above the seafloor, powerful physics takes over. Where the fast, warm Gulf Stream slams into Atlantic storms, sudden thirty-meter rogue waves can overwhelm steel cargo ships in minutes—like the SS Cotopaxi, lost in 1925 and finally identified underwater decades later.

Some once blamed giant seafloor methane eruptions for sinking ships. While dense gas plumes can reduce buoyancy in lab tanks, U.S. Geological Survey records show no massive hydrate blowouts have struck this region in fifteen thousand years.

Yet seismologists did uncover a real anomaly deeper underground.

Directly beneath Bermuda's crust sits an unusual twenty-kilometer-thick layer of frozen mantle rock—a hidden geological raft holding the surrounding seafloor five hundred meters higher than normal.

According to NOAA, ships don't disappear here more often than in any other stormy shipping lane. What hides beneath the Bermuda Triangle isn't folklore—it's extreme ocean physics and an eight-kilometer abyss.

## Factual Sources Supporting the Narration

1. **NOAA National Ocean Service — What is the Bermuda Triangle? & Puerto Rico Trench Bathymetry (Milwaukee Deep ~8,376 m).** — https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/bermudatri.html
   - *Supports:* No evidence that mysterious disappearances occur with greater frequency in the Bermuda Triangle than in any other large, well-traveled area of the ocean; Gulf Stream rapid weather/wave changes; deep trenches including the Puerto Rico Trench.
2. **U.S. Geological Survey (USGS) — Gas Hydrates and the Bermuda Triangle Myth.** — https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-bermuda-triangle
   - *Supports:* USGS gas-hydrate research shows no large methane-hydrate releases have occurred in the Bermuda Triangle / Blake Ridge region for roughly the past 15,000 years.
3. **Frazer, W. D., Park, J., & Olugboji, T. — Seismic receiver-function imaging of a ~20-km-thick low-density underplated rock layer beneath Bermuda supporting the ~500-m Bermuda Swell.** — https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/19448007
   - *Supports:* Identification of an unusual ~20-kilometer-thick underplated rock layer beneath Bermuda's oceanic crust whose buoyancy holds the surrounding Bermuda seafloor swell ~500 meters higher than expected without an active hot-spot plume.
4. **NOAA Ocean Exploration — Identification of the Wreck of SS Cotopaxi off St. Augustine, Florida (2020).** — https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/
   - *Supports:* SS Cotopaxi vanished on a voyage from Charleston to Havana in December 1925 during a severe Atlantic storm and was positively identified underwater ~35 nautical miles off St. Augustine, Florida.
