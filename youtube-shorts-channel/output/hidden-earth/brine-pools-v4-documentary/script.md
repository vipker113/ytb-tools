## Verbatim Final Spoken Narration

A kilometer down on the pitch-black ocean floor, deep-sea cameras cross a literal shoreline—into an underwater lake.

Waves ripple across its mirror-like surface, while dark streams pour down the seabed slope.

Its secret is buried ancient salt. Dissolving beneath the Gulf of Mexico and the Red Sea, it creates brine up to eight times saltier than seawater.

Too heavy to mix, that dense brine pools in seafloor basins—trapping toxic methane and sulfur with zero oxygen.

Any crab or fish that strays across the rim suffers instant anoxic shock. Yet dense beds of mussels and tubeworms thrive right along that deadly edge.

In 2022, scientists dove 1,770 meters into the Red Sea—and pulled mud cores from untouched brine pools. Without burrowing animals, those layers preserve a thousand years of earthquakes and tsunamis.

Researchers also test these chemical boundaries as stand-ins for icy moons like Europa. Of course, that doesn't prove life exists beyond Earth.

Deep-sea brine pools aren't alien portals. They're toxic underwater lakes—recording Earth's violent past and fueling life in total darkness.
