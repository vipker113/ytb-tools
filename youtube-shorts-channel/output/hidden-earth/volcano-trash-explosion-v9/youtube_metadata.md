## YouTube Description (Ready to Copy-Paste — Includes Required Media Credits)

Lava lakes glow past 1,100°C—so why doesn't humanity dump its 2 billion annual tons of municipal garbage straight into active volcanoes?

In 2002, field researchers at Ethiopia's Erta Ale volcano dropped a 30-kilogram bag of camp waste onto a crusted basaltic lava lake. Rather than sinking harmlessly, the bag pierced the cooled crust and triggered a violent fountain of molten spatter: trapped moisture inside organic waste flashes instantaneously into steam, expanding roughly 1,600 times in volume beneath viscous magma.

Even worse, most lava on Earth is basaltic (~1,100–1,200°C), cooler than the melting point of structural steels (~1,370–1,540°C), while burning plastics and electronics in an open crater vents unfiltered dioxins, heavy metals, and acid gases directly into the atmosphere—unlike sealed waste-to-energy reactors equipped with multi-stage chemical scrubbers. Fewer than ten volcanoes worldwide even host persistent lava lakes, and pressurized magma rises upward rather than draining downward.
