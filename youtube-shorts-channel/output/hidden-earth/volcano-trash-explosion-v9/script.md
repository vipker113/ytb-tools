## Verbatim Final Spoken Narration

Lava lakes burn past eleven hundred degrees Celsius—so why don't we dump all our trash into volcanoes?

In 2002, researchers at Ethiopia's Erta Ale volcano dropped a thirty-kilogram bag of camp waste onto a crusted lava lake. Instead of vanishing quietly, the crust ruptured into a violent fountain of molten rock.

Household trash holds trapped moisture that flashes into steam, expanding sixteen hundred times in volume beneath the crust. Worse, basalt lava hovers near twelve hundred degrees Celsius—too cool to melt steel, which simply floats and smolders.

Burning plastics and electronics in an open crater blasts unfiltered dioxins, heavy metals, and acid gases skyward. Modern waste-to-energy plants prevent that fallout using sealed thermal reactors and chemical scrubbers.

Even if emissions vanished, fewer than ten volcanoes on Earth hold persistent lava lakes, and pressurized magma flows upward—not down. Dump two billion tons of waste inside, and Earth wouldn't swallow our garbage—it would fire it back.
