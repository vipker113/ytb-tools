## YouTube Description (Ready to Copy-Paste — Includes Required Media Credits)

Along the Mid-Atlantic Ridge—at sites such as TAG (3,600 m), Snake Pit (3,500 m), and Lucky Strike (1,700 m)—hydrothermal "black smoker" chimneys blast superheated 350–366°C mineral-rich water straight into 2°C abyssal seawater without boiling into steam. Crushing hydrostatic pressure (up to ~360 atmospheres) keeps the fluid liquid until dissolved iron and sulfide minerals precipitate on contact with freezing ocean water, building multi-story sulfide spires.

Inches from water hot enough to melt lead, up to 2,500 pale blind shrimp (Rimicaris exoculata) crowd every square meter in pitch darkness. Lacking normal eyeballs, they carry a reflective white dorsal photoreceptor organ across their backs tuned to the faint thermal glow of scalding vents—allowing them to farm sulfur-oxidizing bacteria inside their gill chambers without drifting into lethal heat.
